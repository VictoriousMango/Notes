Fourier Optics

Think of this as the “frequency-domain language” of optics. If you understand these cards, you can reason about most Fourier Optics VIVA questions: from Fraunhofer diffraction and spatial filtering to OTF/MTF and sampling.

Fourier Transform Fundamentals

Fourier representation of optical fields, spatial frequencies and the link between object domain (x, y) and frequency domain (fₓ, fᵧ).

Definition

Fourier Transform of a Field

Any reasonably well-behaved 2D optical field can be decomposed into spatial frequency components via the Fourier transform.

\[ F(f_x, f_y) = \iint f(x, y)\, e^{-i 2\pi (f_x x + f_y y)} \, dx\,dy \]
  • f(x, y): field or amplitude in the spatial domain.
  • F(fₓ, fᵧ): distribution of spatial frequencies.
  • High |f| → rapid spatial variation (fine details/edges).

Physical meaning

Spatial Frequency Interpretation

Spatial frequency is the “cycles per unit distance” in the image or object.

  • Low frequencies: smooth variations, overall shape.
  • High frequencies: edges, fine textures, sharp transitions.
  • Finite aperture → low-pass filter in spatial frequency.

Linear systems

Convolution & Multiplication Duality

For linear shift-invariant systems, convolution in space ↔ multiplication in frequency.

y(x, y) = x(x, y) * h(x, y)
Y(fₓ, fᵧ) = X(fₓ, fᵧ) · H(fₓ, fᵧ)
  • h(x, y): impulse response (PSF), H: transfer function (OTF).
  • Design often easier in frequency domain (filter masks, OTF shaping).

Diffraction Theory

Fresnel and Fraunhofer diffraction as propagation of fields, and their connection to Fourier transforms of apertures.

Scalar theory

Huygens–Fresnel & Kirchhoff Integral

Every point on a wavefront acts as a secondary spherical wavelet; the field at a point is the superposition of all contributions.

  • Kirchhoff integral is a scalar approximation to Maxwell’s equations.
  • Assumes paraxial regime and certain boundary conditions.
  • Forms basis for Fresnel and Fraunhofer diffraction formulas.

Near vs far field

Fresnel vs Fraunhofer Diffraction

Whether we are in near or far field depends on the Fresnel number:

NF = a² / (λ z)
  • Fresnel (near field): NF ≳ 1; quadratic phase terms matter.
  • Fraunhofer (far field): NF ≪ 1; pattern is FT of aperture.
  • Fraunhofer often reached using a lens and finite distance.

Examples

Canonical Diffraction Patterns

Common patterns are direct Fourier transforms of simple apertures.

  • Single slit → sinc² pattern.
  • Circular aperture → Airy disk (Bessel function behaviour).
  • Gratings → combs of sharp diffraction orders in frequency space.

Coherent vs Incoherent Imaging

How imaging differs when illumination is coherent (fields add) vs incoherent (intensities add), and what that implies for system transfer.

Coherent

Coherent Imaging System Model

Under coherent illumination, complex field amplitudes superpose.

  • Image field = object field convolved with coherent PSF.
  • Transfer function is pupil function (with phase).
  • High sensitivity to phase errors and aberrations.

Incoherent

Incoherent Imaging System Model

With incoherent illumination, intensities superpose, not fields.

  • Image intensity = object intensity convolved with intensity PSF.
  • Transfer function is OTF (Fourier transform of intensity PSF).
  • Aberrations appear as reduction in MTF, not simple pupil phase.

Comparison

Key Differences: Coherent vs Incoherent

Same optical system behaves differently depending on coherence.

  • Coherent: linear in complex amplitude; OTF = pupil.
  • Incoherent: linear in intensity; OTF = autocorrelation of pupil.
  • Incoherent imaging generally gives smoother, less speckled images.

OTF, MTF & System Response

Optical transfer function, modulation transfer function and phase transfer function as metrics of imaging quality in the frequency domain.

Definition

Optical Transfer Function (OTF)

OTF describes how each spatial frequency in the object is transferred to the image.

OTF(fₓ, fᵧ) = FT{ PSF(x, y) }
  • For incoherent imaging: PSF is intensity impulse response.
  • OTF(0, 0) = 1 by normalization.
  • Complex-valued: has magnitude and phase.

Magnitude & phase

MTF & PTF

Decompose OTF into magnitude and phase:

  • MTF (Modulation Transfer Function) = |OTF|.
  • PTF (Phase Transfer Function) = arg(OTF).
  • MTF tells how contrast is transferred; PTF tells about phase distortion.

Cutoff

Cutoff Frequency & Resolution

The finite pupil of an imaging system limits the highest spatial frequency it can pass.

  • Incoherent diffraction-limited cutoff: fc ≈ 2 NA / λ.
  • Coherent diffraction-limited cutoff: fc ≈ NA / λ.
  • MTF roll-off shape indicates practical resolution and contrast.

Sampling, Digital Optics & Holography

Relation between continuous fields and sampled data, aliasing, pixel pitch, and basics of digital holography & Fourier optics implementation.

Nyquist

Nyquist Sampling in Space

To reconstruct a band-limited image without aliasing, sample at least twice the highest spatial frequency present.

fs ≥ 2 fmax
  • Pixel pitch p ⇒ sampling frequency fs = 1/p.
  • Aliasing: high frequencies masquerade as lower ones.
  • Optical prefiltering (aperture/MTF) interacts with detector sampling.

Discrete FT

Discrete Fourier Transform (DFT) in Optics

Numerical propagation and digital holography use DFT/FFT to approximate propagation integrals.

  • Sampling grid in x–y maps to discrete grid in fₓ–fᵧ.
  • Zero padding controls interpolation and sampling in frequency domain.
  • Scaling factors set by pixel size and wavelength.

Digital holography

Digital Holography Workflow

Record interference fringes using a sensor; reconstruct object numerically.

  • Record intensity of object + reference beams.
  • Use numerical Fresnel/Fourier propagation to reconstruct amplitude/phase.
  • Enables numerical refocusing and phase imaging.

Applications & Advanced Topics

Spatial filtering, 4f correlators, optical information processing, and multi-aperture / interferometric systems.

4f system

4f Spatial Filtering System

A pair of lenses separated by 2f + 2f forms a 4f system that maps object → frequency plane → image.

  • Object at front focal plane of lens 1.
  • Fourier plane at common focal plane between lenses.
  • Spatial filter (aperture / mask) placed in Fourier plane to modify frequencies.

Correlation

Optical Correlator (Matched Filtering)

Optical correlators implement pattern recognition by multiplying spectra and inverse Fourier transforming.

  • Correlation in space ↔ multiplication with complex conjugate spectrum.
  • Bright spot in output indicates match between object and reference.
  • Used in target recognition and optical signal processing.

Multi-aperture

Multi-Aperture & Interferometric Systems

Multiple sub-apertures combine to synthesize a larger effective aperture in frequency space.

  • Baseline spacing ↔ spatial frequency coverage.
  • Used in astronomical interferometry to achieve very high resolution.
  • Fourier coverage (u–v plane) determines image quality & reconstruction.
