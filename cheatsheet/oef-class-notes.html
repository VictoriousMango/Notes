Ray & Wave Optics Fundamentals

Postulates of ray optics, Fermat’s principle, ABCD matrices, wave equation and Fresnel / paraxial approximations.

Ray optics

Ray Postulates & Fermat’s Principle

Ray optics assumes light travels in straight lines in homogeneous media and bends at interfaces according to Snell’s law.

  • Refractive index: n = c / v.
  • Fermat: actual path extremizes optical path length ∫n ds.
  • Snell: n₁ sinθ₁ = n₂ sinθ₂; TIR when θ > θc = sin⁻¹(n₂/n₁).

Matrix optics

ABCD Matrix & Imaging Condition

Paraxial rays are represented by height and angle vectors transformed by 2×2 ABCD matrices.

  • [y₂ θ₂]ᵀ = M [y₁ θ₁]ᵀ; det M = 1 for lossless optical systems.
  • C = 0 at image plane; system focal length from matrix elements.
  • Thin lens: 1/f = 1/g + 1/b; thick lens uses principal planes and effective f.

Wave optics

Wave Equation, Helmholtz & Paraxial Approx.

Optical fields satisfy the scalar wave equation and, for monochromatic fields, the Helmholtz equation.

  • ∇²U + k²U = 0 with k = 2π/λ.
  • Slowly varying envelope → paraxial Helmholtz: (∇⊥² + 2jk ∂/∂z)A = 0.
  • Fresnel number F = a²/(λz) distinguishes near vs far-field regimes.

Two-Beam Interference & Interferometers

Classic two-beam formulas, visibility and the basic interferometer families.

2-beam

Two-Beam Interference & Visibility

Superposition of two monochromatic beams with intensities I₁ and I₂ and phase difference Δφ gives:

  • I = I₁ + I₂ + 2√(I₁I₂) cosΔφ.
  • Visibility V = (Imax − Imin)/(Imax + Imin), 0 ≤ V ≤ 1.
  • Max contrast when I₁ = I₂ and fields are fully coherent.

Setups

Michelson, Mach–Zehnder & Fabry–Perot

Different interferometer geometries encode path difference in different ways.

  • Michelson: single beam splitter, two arms, recombination for OPD scan.
  • Mach–Zehnder: two splitters, separate output ports, good for phase modulation.
  • Fabry–Perot: multiple-beam interference in a cavity → high spectral resolution.

Gyro

Sagnac Interferometer (Rotation Sensing)

A loop interferometer where rotation changes optical path length in opposite directions.

  • Phase shift ∝ enclosed area × rotation rate.
  • Used in fiber-optic gyroscopes and ring laser gyros.

Multiple-Wave Interference & Speckle

Interference of many waves, Fabry–Perot finesse and statistics of laser speckle.

M waves

Interference of M Equal-Amplitude Waves

M coherent waves with equal amplitude and phase step φ form a phasor polygon.

  • Resultant E ∝ sin(Mφ/2)/sin(φ/2).
  • Peak intensity ∝ M²I₀, average ∝ MI₀.
  • Basis for sharp transmission peaks in multi-beam interferometers.

Fabry–Perot

Fabry–Perot Finesse & Resolution

In Fabry–Perot, many internal reflections create a geometric series in amplitude.

  • Transmission shows sharp resonances separated by FSR (free spectral range).
  • Finesse ℱ = FSR / FWHM; larger finesse → higher spectral resolution.
  • Reflectivity of mirrors controls finesse and peak transmission.

Speckle

Speckle Formation & Statistics

Speckle arises from interference of many scattered coherent wavelets from a rough surface.

  • Random phasor sum → amplitude Rayleigh distributed, intensity exponential.
  • Mean intensity ⟨I⟩, PDF p(I) = (1/⟨I⟩)exp(−I/⟨I⟩) for fully developed speckle.
  • Used in ESPI, metrology and imaging through scattering media.

Theory of Optical Coherence

Random fields, temporal & spatial coherence, correlation functions and Wiener–Khinchin theorem.

Temporal

Temporal Coherence & Coherence Time

Temporal coherence describes how well a field correlates with itself after a delay τ.

  • Autocorrelation: G(τ) = ⟨U*(t)U(t+τ)⟩.
  • Normalized degree: g(τ) = G(τ)/G(0).
  • Coherence time τc where |g(τ)| drops to 1/e (or 1/2); coherence length lc = cτc.

Spectra

Power Spectral Density & Wiener–Khinchin

The temporal spectrum and autocorrelation are Fourier-transform pairs.

  • S(ν) = ℱ{G(τ)}; G(τ) = ℱ⁻¹{S(ν)}.
  • Average intensity: ⟨I⟩ = ∫S(ν)dν.
  • Narrow spectrum ⇔ long coherence time; wide spectrum ⇔ short τc.

Spatial

Spatial Coherence & Mutual Intensity

Spatial coherence measures correlation between fields at two points in space.

  • Mutual coherence: G(r₁,r₂,τ); at τ=0 → mutual intensity J(r₁,r₂).
  • Degree of coherence μ(r₁,r₂) = J / √(I₁I₂).
  • Coherence area: region where |μ| remains high; hot surface ~ λ² scale.

Applications of Coherence & Interferometry

FT spectroscopy, OCT, extended sources, van Cittert–Zernike and stellar interferometry.

FTS

Fourier-Transform Spectroscopy

A Michelson interferometer records intensity vs delay, whose Fourier transform gives the spectrum.

  • Interferogram I(τ) ↔ spectral density S(ν).
  • High resolution from long travel and phase control, not narrow slits.

OCT

Optical Coherence Tomography (OCT)

Uses low-coherence interferometry to depth-resolve scattering layers.

  • Short coherence length → interference only when path lengths match within lc.
  • Time-domain / swept-source / spectral-domain variants.
  • Key tool in biomedical imaging & NDT.

VCZ & stars

van Cittert–Zernike & Stellar Interferometry

For an incoherent extended source, spatial coherence is FT of source intensity.

  • Visibility vs baseline encodes source angular size.
  • Michelson stellar interferometer: first zero of visibility gives θ ∼ 1.22 λ/d.

Gaussian Beams & Modes

Paraxial Helmholtz solutions, q-parameter, beam waist, divergence and HG/LG modes.

q-parameter

q-Parameter & Rayleigh Range

The fundamental Gaussian beam is described by a complex q(z):

  • q(z) = z + jz₀, where z₀ = πW₀²/λ is the Rayleigh range.
  • Beam radius: W(z) = W₀√(1 + (z/z₀)²).
  • Divergence: θ ≈ λ/(πW₀); depth of focus (confocal parameter) = 2z₀.

Phase

Radius of Curvature & Gouy Phase

The Gaussian beam has curved wavefronts and an extra phase term.

  • R(z) = z[1 + (z₀/z)²]; R → ∞ at waist, R ≈ z far away.
  • Gouy phase: ζ(z) = arctan(z/z₀), total shift π across focus.

Modes

Hermite- & Laguerre-Gaussian Modes

Paraxial equation admits higher-order transverse modes.

  • HGₘₙ(x,y,z) with Hermite polynomials in Cartesian coordinates.
  • LGₚˡ(ρ,φ,z) with Laguerre polynomials and azimuthal phase e^{jℓφ} (OAM beams).
  • Used in optical tweezers and mode engineering.

Polarization & Representations

Polarization states, Poincaré sphere, Jones & Stokes descriptions and basic polarizing components.

States

Polarization States & Ellipse

Polarization is the trajectory of the tip of the electric field vector at a point.

  • Linear: components in phase or π out of phase.
  • Circular: equal amplitudes, ±π/2 phase → left/right circular.
  • Elliptical: general case; described by orientation and ellipticity angles.

Jones / Stokes

Jones & Stokes Descriptions

Two complementary ways to represent polarization.

  • Jones vector J = [Aₓ, Aᵧ]ᵀ for fully polarized, coherent fields.
  • Stokes vector S = (S₀,S₁,S₂,S₃) for both fully and partially polarized light.
  • Mueller 4×4 matrices act on Stokes; Jones 2×2 matrices act on Jones vectors.

Components

Polarizers & Waveplates

Anisotropic materials control amplitude and phase of polarization components.

  • Polarizers: dichroic, Brewster-angle, birefringent prism types.
  • Retarders: phase delay Δφ = (2π/λ)d(nₑ − nₒ).
  • λ/2 plate rotates linear polarization by 2θ; λ/4 at 45° converts linear ↔ circular.

Holography

Recording and reconstruction of complex wavefronts, in-line vs off-axis, image/Fourier/volume holograms.

Principle

Recording & Reconstruction

Holography stores interference between object and reference waves.

  • Record intensity ∝ |U₀ + Uᵣ|² on a medium → contains amplitude & phase via cross terms.
  • Re-illuminate with reference (or conjugate) to reconstruct object wavefront.

Geometries

In-line vs Off-Axis Holography

Geometry determines how object and conjugate images overlap.

  • In-line (Gabor): reference along same direction → twin-image problem.
  • Off-axis (Leith–Upatnieks): reference at angle; separates orders spatially.

Types

Image, Fourier & Volume Holograms

Holograms can be recorded in different planes and media.

  • Image hologram: recording near image plane; reconstruction gives image directly.
  • Fourier hologram: recording at FT plane; useful for correlation and filtering.
  • Volume hologram: thick medium, Bragg selectivity; obeys 2d sinφ = mλ.

Microscopy & Imaging Systems

4-f systems, pupils & stops, conventional vs phase-contrast vs confocal microscopy.

4-f

4-f Imaging & Spatial Filtering

Two lenses in 4-f arrangement implement Fourier transform and inverse transform.

  • Object at front focal plane of lens 1; FT in the common focal plane.
  • Spatial filters in frequency plane control resolution and noise.

Microscopes

Bright-Field & Phase-Contrast Microscopy

Modifying phase and amplitude at the pupil reveals weak phase objects.

  • Bright-field: standard illumination, contrast from absorption/scattering.
  • Phase-contrast: phase ring + annular illumination converts phase shifts to intensity.

Confocal

Confocal Microscopy & Resolution

Confocal adds a pinhole conjugate to focus to reject out-of-focus light.

  • Improved axial sectioning: z-res ≈ λ/2(NA)², lateral ≈ λ/2NA.
  • 3D imaging via z-stack and reconstruction.
