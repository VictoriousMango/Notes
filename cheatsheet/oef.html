Wave & Electromagnetic Optics

Wave equation, EM description of light, paraxial approximation, reflection/refraction and basic wave propagation.

Wave equation

EM Wave Equation in Free Space

Light is a transverse electromagnetic wave; electric and magnetic fields satisfy the same wave equation.

∇²E − (1/c²) ∂²E/∂t² = 0
  • Solution: plane waves, Gaussian beams, spherical waves, etc.
  • Speed: c = 1/√(μ₀ε₀).
  • In media: c → v = c/n, where n is refractive index.

Approximation

Paraxial Approximation

For rays making small angles with the optical axis, sinθ ≈ θ and tanθ ≈ θ.

  • Simplifies ray and wave equations → Gaussian optics.
  • Valid for narrow-angle systems with small field of view.
  • Breaks down for high-NA, wide-field, or strongly focusing systems.

Interfaces

Reflection, Refraction & Snell’s Law

At a plane interface, tangential components of E and H are continuous, leading to Snell’s law and Fresnel coefficients.

  • Snell: n₁ sinθ₁ = n₂ sinθ₂.
  • Fresnel coefficients → amplitude reflection / transmission.
  • Special cases: Brewster angle, total internal reflection.

Interference, Diffraction & Coherence

Conditions for interference, temporal/spatial coherence, and Fraunhofer/Fresnel diffraction ideas.

Interference

Conditions for Observable Interference

Stable interference fringes require a fixed phase relationship between waves.

  • Same frequency (or very close).
  • Temporal coherence: path difference < coherence length.
  • Spatial coherence: points on wavefront must maintain phase correlation.

Coherence

Temporal vs Spatial Coherence

Coherence describes the correlation of a field with itself in time and space.

  • Temporal: how long phase remains predictable → coherence time τc, length Lc = cτc.
  • Spatial: correlation between two points on a wavefront → affects fringe contrast in Young’s experiment.
  • Lasers: high temporal & spatial coherence; LEDs: low temporal, moderate spatial.

Diffraction

Fresnel vs Fraunhofer Diffraction

Both describe bending and spreading of waves; differ in distance regime.

  • Fresnel: near field; quadratic phase variation significant.
  • Fraunhofer: far field; aperture field’s Fourier transform.
  • Physical optics uses Fresnel number to classify regime.

Matrix Optics & Gaussian Ray Optics

ABCD matrices, ray vectors, and Gaussian beam parameters like waist, radius of curvature and divergence.

ABCD

Ray Transfer (ABCD) Matrix

Paraxial propagation through an optical system can be described by a 2×2 matrix:

\[ \begin{bmatrix} y_2 \\ \theta_2 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} A & B \\ C & D \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} y_1 \\ \theta_1 \end{bmatrix} \]
  • Free space: A = 1, B = L; C = 0, D = 1.
  • Thin lens: A = 1, B = 0; C = −1/f, D = 1.
  • Multiply matrices in order of traversal.

Gaussian beam

Gaussian Beam Parameters

TEM₀₀ beam has a Gaussian intensity profile with minimum spot size at the waist.

  • Waist radius: w₀ (minimum beam size).
  • Rayleigh range: zR = π w₀² / λ.
  • Divergence: θ ≈ λ / (π w₀) (far field).
  • Spot size: w(z) = w₀ √(1 + (z / zR)²).

q-parameter

Complex q-Parameter

Gaussian beams through ABCD systems use the complex beam parameter q.

1 / q(z) = 1 / R(z) − i λ / (π w²(z))
  • Transforms as q₂ = (A q₁ + B) / (C q₁ + D).
  • R(z): radius of curvature; w(z): spot size.
  • Used to design stable resonators & beam expanders.

Polarization

Jones vectors and matrices, Stokes parameters, wave plates and partial polarization.

Jones

Jones Vector & Jones Matrix

For fully polarized, monochromatic light, the transverse electric field is represented as a 2-element complex vector:

\[ \mathbf{E} = \begin{bmatrix} E_x \\ E_y \end{bmatrix} \]
  • Optical elements → 2×2 Jones matrices.
  • Example: ideal linear polarizer, QWP, HWP.
  • Output: Eout = J · Ein.

Stokes

Stokes Parameters

Stokes vector represents partially polarized light using 4 real numbers:

S = [ I, Q, U, V ]ᵀ
  • I: total intensity.
  • Q, U: linear polarization components.
  • V: circular polarization.
  • Degree of polarization: P = √(Q² + U² + V²) / I.

Waveplates

Quarter & Half Wave Plates

Birefringent elements introduce a phase delay between orthogonal components.

  • QWP: δ = π/2 → converts linear ↔ circular (for 45° input).
  • HWP: δ = π → rotates linear polarization by 2θ.
  • Used for polarization control, retarders in AO, etc.

Fourier Optics & Holography

Convolution, spatial frequency, OTF/MTF, lens Fourier transforms, and hologram basics.

System model

Convolution in Imaging Systems

Linear, shift-invariant imaging system → image is convolution of object with PSF.

Image(x, y) = Object(x, y) * PSF(x, y)
  • PSF: response to point source.
  • Blurring = PSF broad; sharp image = PSF narrow.
  • Fourier domain: multiplication by OTF.

OTF

Optical Transfer Function & MTF

OTF is the system’s response to spatial frequencies.

OTF(fₓ, fᵧ) = FT{ PSF(x, y) }
  • Magnitude → MTF: contrast transfer.
  • Phase → PTF: phase distortions.
  • Cutoff spatial frequency set by NA and λ.

Lens FT

Lens as Fourier Transform Element

Object at front focal plane → back focal plane field ∝ Fourier transform of object field.

  • Used in spatial filtering (4f systems).
  • Holography & optical information processing.
  • Frequency-domain interpretation of imaging.

Holography

Digital Holography Concept

Interference pattern recorded by a sensor and numerically reconstructed by digital processing.

  • Record: object + reference beams → fringe pattern.
  • Reconstruct: numerically propagate field (Fresnel/Fourier methods).
  • Allows refocusing and phase retrieval from recorded intensity.

Photon Optics / Quantum Optics

Photons, Bohr model, wave–particle duality and basic quantum ideas used in optics.

Photon

Photon: Energy & Momentum

Light energy is quantized in units called photons.

E = hν = hc / λ ,  p = h / λ
  • Explains photoelectric effect and line spectra.
  • Zero rest mass, always travels at speed c in vacuum.
  • Photon number fluctuations → shot noise.

Duality

Wave–Particle Duality

Light shows both wave-like and particle-like behavior depending on the experiment.

  • Interference/diffraction → wave nature.
  • Photoelectric & Compton → particle nature.
  • De Broglie: λ = h / p for matter waves.

Bohr

Bohr Model of Hydrogen

Electron orbits in discrete energy levels; radiation occurs during transitions.

  • Angular momentum: mvr = nħ.
  • Energy levels: Eₙ ∝ −1/n².
  • Line spectra: ΔE = hν between two orbits.

Nonlinear Optics

Intensity-dependent refractive index, harmonic generation, phase matching and basic χ⁽²⁾/χ⁽³⁾ processes.

Polarization

Nonlinear Polarization Expansion

In strong fields, polarization is not proportional to E:

P = ε₀(χ¹E + χ²E² + χ³E³ + …)
  • χ¹ → linear optics.
  • χ² → second-harmonic, sum/difference frequency generation.
  • χ³ → Kerr effect, self-focusing, four-wave mixing.

SHG

Second Harmonic Generation (SHG)

Two photons at frequency ω combine to form one photon at 2ω in a χ² medium.

  • Requires phase matching to be efficient.
  • Common in nonlinear crystals (KDP, BBO, etc.).
  • Used for frequency doubling of lasers (e.g., 1064 nm → 532 nm).

Kerr

Kerr Effect & Self-phase Modulation

Intensity-dependent refractive index:

n(I) = n₀ + n₂ I
  • Leads to self-focusing and self-phase modulation.
  • Important in ultrafast and fiber nonlinear optics.

Imaging & Instruments

Eye model, microscopes, telescopes, resolution and radiometry basics.

Eye

Optical Model of the Human Eye

The eye is a variable-focus optical system with cornea + lens forming an image on the retina.

  • Effective focal length ≈ 17 mm (in aqueous).
  • Accommodation: changing lens curvature to focus.
  • Defects: myopia, hypermetropia, astigmatism → corrected by spectacles.

Microscope

Microscope Resolution

Resolution limited by diffraction and NA of objective.

dmin ≈ 0.61 λ / NA
  • NA = n sinθ, where n: immersion medium, θ: half-angle of collection.
  • Higher NA & shorter λ → higher resolution.

Radiometry

Radiometric Basics

Quantities describing optical power and brightness.

  • Power (Φ) in watts.
  • Irradiance (E): power per area.
  • Radiance (L): power per area per solid angle → conserved in lossless systems.

Application / Cross-Topic Questions

“Why” questions that combine multiple concepts: scattering, SAR imaging, atmospheric effects, etc.

Scattering

Why Does Scattering Dominate in Biological Tissues?

Refractive index inhomogeneities at the micron scale cause strong scattering.

  • Multiple scattering → loss of ballistic photons.
  • Absorption often weaker in visible/NIR compared to scattering.
  • Key in biomedical optics and imaging depth limits.

Thin films

Color in Thin Films

Results from wavelength-dependent interference between reflections at film interfaces.

  • Path difference: 2 n t cosθ inside film.
  • Constructive / destructive interference for different λ → colors.
  • Used in AR coatings and filters.

Twinkling

Why Do Stars Twinkle but Planets Don’t?

Atmospheric turbulence creates time-varying phase screens.

  • Stars ≈ point sources → intensity fluctuates strongly.
  • Planets subtend larger apparent size → fluctuations average out.
  • AO systems are designed to correct similar atmospheric effects.

Extremely Advanced / Research-Level

Metasurfaces, photonic crystals, transformation optics and optical cloaking – high-level summaries.

Metasurfaces

Metasurfaces & Wavefront Engineering

Planar arrays of subwavelength scatterers that locally control phase, amplitude and polarization.

  • Act like “flat lenses”, beam shapers, holograms.
  • Design uses effective medium / resonant scatterer models.

Photonic crystals

Photonic Crystals & Bandgaps

Periodic modulation of refractive index → photonic band structure (analogous to electrons in solids).

  • Photonic bandgap: frequency range where propagation is forbidden.
  • Used for waveguiding, high-Q cavities, and light localization.

Cloaking

Transformation Optics & Cloaking (Conceptual)

Uses coordinate transformations to design spatially varying ε and μ that guide light around objects.

  • Requires anisotropic, inhomogeneous metamaterials.
  • Practical cloaks are limited in bandwidth and viewing angle.
